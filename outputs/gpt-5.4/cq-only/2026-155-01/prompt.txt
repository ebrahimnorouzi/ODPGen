You are an ontology engineer. Generate an OWL ontology design pattern (ODP) in Turtle syntax that supports the competency questions below.

Requirements:
- Model only what is needed to answer these CQs; do not add concepts not implied by them.
- Use clear, self-explanatory class and property names.
- Add rdfs:label and rdfs:comment annotations to every class and property.
- Declare a mandatory ontology header: `:Ontology a owl:Ontology ; rdfs:label "..." ; rdfs:comment "..." .`

Use these standard prefixes (declare all that you use):
  @prefix owl:  <http://www.w3.org/2002/07/owl#> .
  @prefix rdf:  <http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#> .
  @prefix rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#> .
  @prefix xsd:  <http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#> .
  @prefix :     <http://example.org/odp#> .

Output format:
1. Turtle ontology wrapped in a ```turtle ... ``` code block.
2. A CQ-to-axiom mapping table: for each CQ, list the classes and properties that enable answering it.

Competency Questions:
- Which survey or investigation activities were performed on a given cultural property or on a specific feature of interest?
- Which method or investigation technique was adopted in a specific survey or investigation activity?
- Which observations were produced during a given survey or investigation activity?
- Which experts participated in the survey or investigation activity, and with which roles?
- Which hardware, software, and technical configurations were used to conduct a specific survey or investigation activity?
- What interpretations or claims are supported by the observations produced during a survey?
- How are post-processed outputs (e.g., interactive images, spectra, datasets) related to the underlying acquisition or observation activities?
- Which acquisition parameters (e.g., exposure time, ISO, aperture) characterised a specific photographic acquisition cycle within a survey?
