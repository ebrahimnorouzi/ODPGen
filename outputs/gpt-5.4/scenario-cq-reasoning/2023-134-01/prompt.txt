You are an ontology engineer. Create an OWL ontology design pattern (ODP) in Turtle syntax by following the explicit reasoning steps below.

Reasoning steps (work through each in order):
1. Extract atomic requirements from the scenario (one sentence per requirement).
2. Propose candidate classes and properties for each requirement.
3. Draft axioms (subclass, domain, range, cardinality, disjointness) for each candidate.
4. For each CQ, check which axioms enable answering it; note any CQs not yet covered.
5. Add or refine axioms to cover any uncovered CQs.
6. Remove any classes or properties not traceable to a requirement or CQ.
7. Output the final ontology.

Constraints:
- Keep modeling minimal and reusable; prefer general patterns over domain-specific facts.
- Do not assert domain facts not implied by the scenario or CQs.
- Add rdfs:label and rdfs:comment annotations to every class and property.
- Declare a mandatory ontology header: `:Ontology a owl:Ontology ; rdfs:label "..." ; rdfs:comment "..." .`

Use these standard prefixes (declare all that you use):
  @prefix owl:  <http://www.w3.org/2002/07/owl#> .
  @prefix rdf:  <http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#> .
  @prefix rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#> .
  @prefix xsd:  <http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#> .
  @prefix :     <http://example.org/odp#> .

Output format:
1. Show your reasoning for steps 1–6 (brief bullet points per step).
2. Turtle ontology wrapped in a ```turtle ... ``` code block.
3. Traceability table: two columns — (a) requirement/CQ → axiom(s), (b) axiom → requirement/CQ.

Scenario:
The paper identifies 7 challenges motivating the need for governance patterns in Solid apps:
C1. Identity of Solid actors and their roles is unknown – Solid users are unaware of who are the entities providing their Pods or the apps they use.
C2. No metadata about Solid infrastructure – Users do not have information on which Solid specification their Pod is running or which functionalities are installed within it.
C3. Availability/Discovery of categories of data – To have granular access to data in Pods, based on their data category, Solid apps need to know if they exist and where are they stored in the Pod.
C4. Pod and app providers do not provide information on their data handling practices – Pods and apps do not provide machine-readable privacy notices nor do they declare what data they need to function.
C5. Users cannot express their privacy policies – Solid users do not have the tools to express their privacy preferences and requirements nor to manage incoming data requests or existing decisions for the use of data.
C6. No logging or record-keeping – No provenance metadata is recorded for accountability in the Pods, e.g., users do not keep consent records, nor information regarding who has accessed their data, what they are doing with it, or changes to their data handling policies.
C7. No legal compliance checks – Solid does not have tools for users to exercise their GDPR-related rights, such as withdrawing consent, nor does it provide authorities with the auditing information to perform investigations.

Competency Questions:
- What is the unique identifier of the policy?
- Who is the creator of the policy?
- When was the policy issued?
- Who is the assignee of the policy?
- What application/service is being used to access the data?
- What access mode is being requested?
- What personal data is being accessed?
- What is the purpose for accessing the data?
