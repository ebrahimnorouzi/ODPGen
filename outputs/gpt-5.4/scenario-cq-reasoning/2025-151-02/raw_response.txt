1. **Reasoning for steps 1–6**

### Step 1. Atomic requirements
- The ontology must represent a policy that is being evaluated.
- The ontology must represent parties relevant to policy evaluation.
- The ontology must represent assets relevant to policy evaluation.
- The ontology must represent collections of assets associated with a policy.
- The ontology must represent collections of parties associated with a policy.
- The ontology must represent rules associated with a policy.
- The ontology must represent actions that were performed or attempted.
- The ontology must represent events that have occurred and information about them.
- The ontology must represent the number of times a rule has been exercised.
- The ontology must represent the duration for which a rule has been exercised.
- The ontology must represent recipients or recipient categories for the result of an exercised rule.
- The ontology must represent whether a financial payment has been made and the amount paid.
- The ontology must represent the current time.
- The ontology must represent the location of a party.
- The ontology must represent a party requesting access to an asset.
- The ontology must represent a segment or part of an asset, such as minutes 4 to 9 of a video stream.
- The ontology must represent a purpose for access, such as academic research.
