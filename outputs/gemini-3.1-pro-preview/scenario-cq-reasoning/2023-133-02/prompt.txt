You are an ontology engineer. Create an OWL ontology design pattern (ODP) in Turtle syntax.

Before writing, reason through these steps internally (do NOT write them out yet):
1. Extract atomic requirements from the scenario (one sentence each).
2. Propose candidate classes and properties per requirement.
3. Draft axioms (subclass, domain, range, cardinality, disjointness).
4. For each CQ, verify which axioms enable answering it; note gaps.
5. Add/refine axioms to cover uncovered CQs.
6. Remove classes/properties not traceable to any requirement or CQ.

Constraints:
- Declare every class with `a owl:Class`, every property with `a owl:ObjectProperty`, `a owl:DatatypeProperty`, or `a owl:AnnotationProperty`.
- Do NOT create individual instances (e.g. `:SomePerson a :Person`); output schema axioms only.
- Add rdfs:label and rdfs:comment to every class and property.
- Declare a mandatory ontology header: `:Ontology a owl:Ontology ; rdfs:label "..." ; rdfs:comment "..." .`
- Keep modeling minimal and reusable; do not assert facts not implied by the scenario or CQs.

Use these standard prefixes (declare all that you use):

Output format — strictly in this order, nothing before item 1:
1. The complete Turtle ontology wrapped in a ```turtle ... ``` code block. Output this FIRST, before any explanation.
2. Reasoning summary: one bullet per step 1–6 (keep each to one line).
3. Traceability table: for each requirement/CQ, list the axiom(s) that cover it.

Scenario:
The use case described in this section is part of the kHealth-Asthma project. Asthma is a complex disease with multiple factors affecting a patient's condition. kHealth-Asthma is a continuous self-monitoring project involving pediatric asthma patients and aiming to understand the effects of outdoor environmental allergens on the patient's symptoms and their medication intake. The data collected about asthma patients include their (1) symptoms, such as cough, chest tightness, wheezing, and breathing patterns, (2) intake of medications, and (3) outdoor environment features, such as pollen and air quality index (AQI). The edges represent the causal relations with effect weights. For example, the presence of outdoor allergen: pollen/AQI causes the patient to experience a cough with causal effect weight "0.8" and "0.4", respectively.

Competency Questions:
- Is the medication intake by the patient caused by the presence of outdoor environmental allergen such as pollen or AQI?
- How much does the presence of an asthma symptom effect the intake of medication?
- How does an outdoor environmental allergen affect the patient?
- What is the probable cause of the cough experienced by the patient?
- What if pollen (or AQI) allergen was not present in the outdoor environment, how would it effect the intake of medication?
- What if the patient did not experience a cough, how would it effect the intake of medication?
