2. A short documentation section with:

**Intent:**  
Model stress detection knowledge in a machine-readable way by linking subjects, stressors, stress states, physiological responses, sensor-based measurements, activity phases, contextual factors, and dataset-specific labels.

**Covered requirements:**
- Represents stressors as events, conditions, or stimuli with the potential to induce stress.
- Represents stress states separately from stressors.
- Represents physiological responses that may indicate stress indirectly.
- Represents sensor measurements as indirect cues rather than direct proof of stress.
- Represents sensors, wearable devices, signal channels, and observable properties.
- Represents contextual factors needed to interpret physiological observations.
- Represents activity phases for experimental and observational context.
- Supports semantic annotation of wearable stress datasets.
- Supports dataset harmonization through dataset-specific labels aligned to shared concepts.
- Preserves distinctions between original dataset labels and shared semantic concepts.
- Represents datasets and experimental protocols as sources of heterogeneity.

**Known assumptions and exclusions:**
- The pattern stays at schema level only and intentionally creates no individuals.
- No automatic equivalence or conflict resolution between dataset labels is asserted.
- Demographics and provenance details are not modeled explicitly because they are only mentioned as examples of dataset differences, not elaborated in the scenario.
- No detailed taxonomy of stress types, sensor types, or physiological signal types is introduced, to remain within scenario scope.
- Datatype modeling for measurements is intentionally minimal and does not include units, timestamps, or quality metrics.
