You are an ontology engineer. Generate an OWL ontology design pattern (ODP) in Turtle syntax from the scenario and competency questions below, then self-validate the output against the checklist.

Use these standard prefixes (declare all that you use):
  @prefix owl:  <http://www.w3.org/2002/07/owl#> .
  @prefix rdf:  <http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#> .
  @prefix rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#> .
  @prefix xsd:  <http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#> .
  @prefix :     <http://example.org/odp#> .

Modeling constraints:
- Do not add concepts or axioms not grounded in the scenario or CQs.
- Declare every class with `a owl:Class`, every property with `a owl:ObjectProperty`, `a owl:DatatypeProperty`, or `a owl:AnnotationProperty`.
- Do NOT create individual instances (e.g. `:SomePerson a :Person`); output schema axioms only.
- Every class and property must have rdfs:label and rdfs:comment annotations.
- Declare a mandatory ontology header: `:Ontology a owl:Ontology ; rdfs:label "..." ; rdfs:comment "..." .`
- Keep the ontology minimal and reusable.

Self-validation checklist (evaluate each item as PASS or FAIL with a one-line reason):
1. Turtle syntax is valid (no missing dots, brackets, or semicolons).
2. All prefixes used in the body are declared at the top.
3. Every property with a domain or range declaration uses only classes defined in this ontology or well-known external vocabularies.
4. No two classes or properties are defined with contradictory restrictions (e.g., disjoint yet subclass, or inconsistent domain declarations).
5. Every CQ is traceable to at least one class or property in the ontology.
6. No concept appears in the ontology that is not grounded in the scenario or CQs.

Output format — strictly in this order, nothing before item 1:
1. The complete Turtle ontology wrapped in a ```turtle ... ``` code block. Output this FIRST, before any explanation.
2. Validation report: checklist items 1–6 each marked PASS or FAIL with a brief reason.
3. Scope report: (a) requirements from the scenario that are covered, (b) any scenario concepts explicitly excluded and why.

Scenario:
Mike needs to solve a task by prompting a foundation model. Thus, he needs a prompting technique suitable for the task. Moreover, the task involves a specific data modality and is subject to different constraints. Another constraint concerns the model architecture, which depends on the available resources. He may also explore model families, variants, and evolutions in order to experiment with prompts. Furthermore, he relies on the analysis of prior chats to design the solution. Specifically, he can identify which models and prompting techniques are most frequently adopted for different tasks. Regarding usage patterns, he may also be interested in statistics on conversation length and in retrieving responses to specific prompts.

Competency Questions:
- What are the tasks?
- What tasks exist for a given data modality?
- What are the constraints of a given task?
- What are the possible foundation model architectures?
- What are the variants and evolutions of a given foundation model?
- What data modalities can a given foundation model process?
- What prompts are based on a given prompting technique?
- What is the response to a given prompt?
- How many prompts and responses are there on average per chat?
- Which foundation model is a given prompt submitted to?
- How many chats were conducted per model?
- Which prompts are based on a prompting technique that is considered suitable for the targeted task?
- What are the prompting techniques?
- What prompting techniques are suitable for a given task?
- What prompting techniques are suitable for tasks targeting a given data modality?
- What prompting techniques are suitable for tasks with a constraint on a given variable?
