### Documentation

**Intent:** 
This ontology design pattern models the semantic management and dynamic allocation of digital assets by representing a matchmaker that evaluates incoming service requests and software requirements against the catalogued characteristics of hardware resources (such as DPUs and GPUs) to identify and allocate suitable execution, support, or deployment candidates.

**Covered requirements:**
*   Representation of a `Catalogue` that describes `DigitalAsset` entities.
*   Taxonomy of digital assets, explicitly distinguishing `Software` and `HardwareResource` (with specific subclasses for `DPU` and `GPU`).
*   Modeling of incoming `ServiceRequest`s that target specific software.
*   Association of `Requirement`s to software and service requests, and `Characteristic`s to digital assets and service requests.
*   Representation of a `Matchmaker` that processes requests, compares requirements and characteristics, and allocates hardware resources.
*   Properties defining how candidate resources interact with the main software (`canExecute`, `canSupport`, `canDeploy`).

**Known assumptions and exclusions:**
*   **Assumptions:** "Candidate resources" are modeled contextually via the `identifiesCandidate` property rather than as a distinct subclass, assuming any `DigitalAsset` can act as a candidate. Union domains are used for `hasCharacteristic` and `hasRequirement` to accurately reflect that both service requests and assets/software can possess them.
*   **Exclusions:** The pattern excludes the internal algorithmic logic of the matchmaker's comparison process. It also excludes broader project context concepts mentioned in the prompt (e.g., "quantum-resilient", "trustworthy AI", "edge computing") as they are motivational background rather than explicit entities interacting within the matchmaker's allocation mechanism.
