### Scope Report
**a) Requirements from the scenario that are covered:**
- **Symbol Table & Renaming:** Covered via `:Symbol`, `:Function`, `:Variable`, `:hasOriginalName`, and `:hasUserDefinedName`.
- **Executable/Binary Files:** Covered via `:ExecutableFile` and its structural relationships (`:hasSymbol`, `:containsAPICall`, `:hasControlFlowPattern`).
- **Malware/Suspicious Behavior:** Covered via `:Activity` subclasses (`:NetworkingActivity`, `:FileSystemActivity`, `:CryptographicActivity`) and boolean flags (`:isSuspicious`, `:isMalicious`).
- **Control Flow & Obfuscation:** Covered via `:ControlFlowPattern` subclasses (`:IndirectControlFlow`, `:DecodeLoop`, `:MemoryJump`) and properties like `:isSelfReferential` and `:indicatesVulnerability`.
- **Entropy/Instruction Analysis:** Covered via `:Instruction`, `:Opcode`, `:totalInstructionCount`, and `:uniqueOpcodeCount`.
- **External Dependencies:** Covered via `:isImported` for functions.

**b) Scenario concepts explicitly excluded and why:**
- **Ghidra (Software Tool):** Excluded because the ontology models the *domain* of reverse engineering (the artifacts and their properties), not the specific proprietary tools used to extract them.
- **Software Supply Chain / Dependency Graph:** While mentioned as a future goal in the scenario ("expand the knowledge graph to apply to finding dependency vulnerabilities in a software supply chain"), the provided CQs focus entirely on the internal analysis of a single executable file. Therefore, supply chain concepts (e.g., `SoftwareBillOfMaterials`, `Vendor`, `ReleaseVersion`) were excluded to keep the ontology minimal and strictly grounded in the current CQs.
- **Entropy Section (as a distinct class):** Excluded because CQ4 specifically asks to detect entropy *through the ratio* of unique opcodes to total instructions in a function. Modeling the counts as datatype properties on the `:Function` class directly answers the CQ without needing an artificial `EntropySection` class.
